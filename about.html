Discrete Mathematics Master Roadmap

Welcome to your comprehensive curriculum guide for Discrete Mathematics. Unlike continuous mathematics (such as calculus), Discrete Math focuses on distinct, separate values, making it the fundamental mathematical language for computer science, programming logic, cryptography, and data structures.

Module 1: Logic and Proofs

The foundation of computer programming and algorithmic reasoning.

  • Propositional Logic: Study of propositions, truth tables, and logical connectives
    (AND, OR, NOT, Implication, Biconditional).
  • Logical Equivalences: Laws of logic and proving equivalence between statements.
  • Predicates and Quantifiers: Universal and Existential quantifiers.
  • Methods of Proof: Direct proof, proof by contradiction, counterexamples, and Mathematical Induction.

Module 2: Sets, Relations, and Functions

Managing data collections, mappings, and relationships.

  • Set Operations: Union, Intersection, Complement, Difference, and Cartesian Product.
  • Set Identities and Venn Diagrams: Visual and algebraic verification of set properties.
  • Relations: Properties of relations including Reflexive, Symmetric, and Transitive properties, plus Equivalence Relations.
  • Functions: One-to-one (Injective), Onto (Surjective), Bijective functions, Inverse functions, and Function Composition.

Module 3: Combinatorics and Counting

Essential for algorithm efficiency analysis, probability, and cryptography.

  • Fundamental Counting Principles: Addition and Multiplication rules.
  • Permutations and Combinations: Ordered arrangements versus selections with or without repetition.
  • Pigeonhole Principle: Core principle for proving guaranteed patterns or overlapping data.
  • Binomial Theorem: Expanding polynomial powers using combinations.
  • >Recurrence Relations: Sequences and recursive formulas (e.g., Fibonacci sequences).

Module 4: Graph Theory and Trees

The math behind networks, routing, social connections, and data structures.

  • Graph Basics: Directed and undirected graphs, vertex degrees, and paths.
  • Special Graphs: Complete graphs, bipartite graphs, and planar graphs.
  • Eulerian and Hamiltonian Paths: Traversing edges and vertices efficiently.
  • Trees: Binary trees, spanning trees, and tree traversals (Preorder, Inorder, Postorder).

Module 5: Boolean Algebra and Logic Circuits

Directly maps to hardware architecture, digital logic, and microprocessor design.

  • Boolean Expressions and Laws: Identity, domination, idempotent, and De Morgan's laws.
  • Logic Gates: Translating logic statements into electronic hardware gates (AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR).
  • Circuit Minimization: Simplifying circuits using Karnaugh Maps (K-Maps).

Module 6: Number Theory and Algorithm Basics

The bedrock of modern cryptography and secure data transmission.

  • Divisibility and Primes: Greatest Common Divisor (GCD), Least Common Multiple (LCM), and the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.
  • Modular Arithmetic: Clock arithmetic and congruences (a ☰ b (mod m)).
  • Euclidean Algorithm: Efficient computation of GCD for large integers.
  • Algorithm Complexity: Introduction to Big-O Notation to measure runtime and efficiency.
